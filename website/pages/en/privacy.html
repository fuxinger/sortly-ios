2. The {{APP_NAME}} app

2.1 Access to your photos

To sort your photos, the app asks for access to your library. You decide whether to share all photos or only selected ones. You can change this at any time in iOS Settings.

The app reads things like capture date, the photos’ GPS data, media type and small previews. In screenshots and documents it recognizes text so you can search for it. All of this happens on your iPhone. The app does not ask for your current location.

Hidden photos are not analyzed by default. The app does not group faces. If this feature is added later, it will stay off until you turn it on, and it will also run only on your device.

2.2 What is stored on your iPhone

The app stores its results in a database on your iPhone: metadata, detected places and trips, recognized text for search, and your decisions. No copies of your photos are made. This database is excluded from iCloud Backup and never leaves your device.

Only a small copy of your decisions is also kept in a separate file that is part of your iPhone backup: rules, exceptions, settings, your own names, the albums the app created and the history of its changes. This way your decisions come back after moving to a new device. The copy contains no photos, no recognized text and no coordinates. Whether and where your iPhone makes backups (iCloud or a computer) is up to you in iOS; I receive nothing.

Deleting: In the app’s settings you can delete and rebuild the analysis. Deleting the app removes all of its data. Albums the app created in the Photos app stay until you remove them or undo them in the app.

2.3 Place names from Apple (can be turned off)

For more accurate city names, the app can use Apple’s location service. iOS then sends single coordinates to Apple, roughly one per area. No photos, names or other data are sent. I don’t receive this data.

This feature is on so that place names are right from the start. You can turn it off at any time in the app’s privacy settings. The app then uses only its built-in offline world map. The legal basis is my legitimate interest in a well-working app (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). See Apple’s Privacy Policy for how Apple processes this data.

2.4 Originals from iCloud (only if you turn it on)

If your photos are stored in iCloud, the app can download originals of screenshots and documents through iOS Photos to recognize text in them. This is off until you allow it, and then works on Wi-Fi only by default. Videos are never downloaded. The transfer happens between your iPhone and your iCloud account at Apple. I don’t receive anything.

2.5 Apple Intelligence

On iPhones with Apple Intelligence, the app can use features like name suggestions for trips. These run on your device. The app does not send any data to me for this.

2.6 Sharing

When you share something, such as your travel atlas as an image, you decide with whom and through which app. What happens next depends on the app you choose.

The same applies to the diagnostics you can share from the app’s settings if something goes wrong. They contain the app and iOS version, the device model, the kind of photo access, and numbers and error codes about recent changes, but no photos, no album or place names and nothing that points to individual photos. You see the text before you send it.

2.7 In-app purchase

Purchases are handled by Apple through the App Store. I don’t receive payment details or your name, only aggregated sales figures from Apple. The app checks on your device whether the full version is unlocked.

2.8 App Store, statistics and crash reports

When you download the app, Apple processes data under its own responsibility. If you have allowed iOS to share analytics with app developers, Apple gives me aggregated usage numbers and crash reports. These contain no photos and don’t identify you. I use them only to fix bugs. You can turn this off under Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements.

2.9 What the app does not do

  • No ads and no tracking. The app doesn’t ask for iOS tracking permission because it doesn’t track.
  • No analytics, advertising or social media SDKs from other companies.
  • No account, no own server, no selling of data.
